Google Antigravity · /swarm

Still asking a single agent to do everything? With the Swarm Sheet, Antigravity’s /swarm splits up the work without the chaos.

A single agent handles one thing at a time and gets lost in a large task. Several agents without an agreement can edit the same file, skip verification, and burn through your quota. The sheet sorts this out before you type /swarm.

Understand it in 5 minutes View on GitHub
In brief

Google added the /swarm command to Antigravity: you describe a task, and a coordinator splits it among several named agents working at the same time, while a verifier reviews and tests their work. This only works when their assignments don’t overlap. The Swarm Sheet is a short plan you write first: who owns what, what each agent must leave alone, what done looks like, who verifies, and when to stop. This guide explains /swarm using the official documentation and includes a free kit that checks your sheet and builds the text to paste. You need a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan for /swarm and Python 3 for the kit.

How /swarm works

One coordinator, several workers, and one verifier on a shared board

Everything in this section comes from the official Google Antigravity documentation. /swarm is available in the app and CLI, is in preview, and is included with Google AI Pro and Ultra plans.

Transparency: We have not tested /swarm. Antigravity is not installed on the machine where this kit was built. This page draws on the official documentation; information from third parties is labeled as a “report.”
You: /swarm + task→ Coordinator splits it up→ Workers @name work together→ @verifier reviews and tests→ Coordinator reports back

🧭 The coordinator

Your conversation becomes the coordinator. It names the swarm, splits the task into non-overlapping pieces (for example, one worker per file or package), and creates named workers such as @parser and @docs, or a numbered group, @fix-1 through @fix-4. It doesn’t do the technical work: it assigns tasks, evaluates the results, and talks with you.

📋 Each worker’s brief

Each worker starts from scratch in its own conversation. Its brief goes on the board, where everyone can see it, and says three things: what it owns, what it must not touch, and what done looks like. That’s exactly what the Swarm Sheet helps you think through first.

🔍 The verifier

The coordinator usually assigns one @verifier for every 2 to 4 workers. It reads the changes (the diffs), runs the tests, and sends issues back to whoever wrote the code. You can ask it to use a different model from the workers.

🗒️ One shared board

Tasks, questions, summaries, and test results all go to a single message board, with mentions like @name, @group, or @all. Workers don’t talk privately: the whole conversation stays in one place, in order.

😴 Waiting doesn’t use tokens

A worker with nothing to do sleeps on the board without using tokens and wakes up when someone mentions it. @monitor stays asleep while there’s progress and only speaks if everyone stops before the work is done.

📁 Workspace

By default, everyone uses your project folder. When several agents edit at the same time, the coordinator can give each one its own working copy. Removing a worker deletes its copy, so it should hand over its changes first.

Which one to use

Subagent, /boost, /swarm, or /teamwork?

A swarm isn’t always the answer. The official table compares four ways to put more than one agent to work. The kit includes a helper that asks these questions for you (how to use it ↓).

Follow along

How to follow the swarm while it works

In the app, the Swarm panel opens beside the conversation through the “Open the Swarm UI” link in the coordinator’s reply. In the CLI, a status line appears below the input field, and the board opens full-screen.

Statuses

  • Active: working.
  • Waiting: asleep on the board without using tokens.
  • Killed: removed; it won’t return, and the coordinator creates another worker.

CLI shortcuts

  • /agents lists the workers.
  • Alt+J jumps to someone awaiting approval; Ctrl+K approves without leaving the conversation.
  • On macOS, Alt+S only works if your terminal sends Option as Alt.

Message a single worker

In the CLI, start with @ and the name: @verifier also run the integration tests. The message goes only to that worker, not to the board. In the app, open its conversation from the Members tab.

The Swarm Sheet

Five questions to answer before typing /swarm

The documentation asks you to say what you want and how to verify it, then lets the coordinator split up the work. The sheet takes one step earlier: you arrive with the assignments already planned, giving the coordinator less chance to send two workers to the same file. Example: a one-page ROI calculator.

TaskSingle-page HTML ROI calculator: enter monthly traffic, conversion rate (%), average order value, and estimated increase (%); get estimated monthly gain, calculated as traffic × (increase/100) × (conversion/100) × order value. Example: 10,000 visits, 2%, R$ 150, and 20% gives R$ 6,000.
How to verifyOne command that proves it worked: node --test tests/calculo.test.js.
Pieces@layout owns the page and styling · @entradas owns the 4 fields · @calculo owns the calculation and test. Each one says what it won’t touch (the other workers’ files) and what done looks like.
VerifierOne, using a different model from the workers: reads the 3 diffs, checks that no one edited another worker’s files, and runs the test.
LimitsOnly 3 workers; if the quota runs out, stop and report it; stop when the test passes or after 2 rounds; remove the workers at the end; don’t run an unsupervised loop.

The kit checks the sheet against 8 items and gives it a score from 0 to 8. It only builds the text to paste into /swarm for a score of 7 or 8; below that, it shows what to fix (how to use it ↓).

Cautions

A swarm can burn through your quota fast. Start small.

Each worker is its own agent. By our count, three workers, one verifier, and the monitor already make five agents, not one.

💳 Quota

The documentation warns that a swarm uses quota much faster than a conversation. It’s in preview and available only on Google AI Pro and Ultra plans.

⏸️ Pause when quota runs out

A member that runs out of quota pauses instead of retrying. It only resumes after a new board post, a model change, or a message from you. It doesn’t restart automatically when the quota renews.

🗑️ Removing a worker is permanent

A removed worker won’t return, and its working copy is deleted. Ask the coordinator to have it hand over its changes first.

🚫 Don’t run unsupervised loops

Report from a user: their account was blocked within minutes when another agent launched dozens of unsupervised sessions within seconds; access returned after an automated appeal. The response quoted in the Google forum said automated use is allowed and counts toward the quota, but high-volume background loops without supervision can trigger anti-abuse protections.

Recommendation: start with 2 or 3 pieces and 1 verifier, follow the board, write down in the sheet how much quota you’re willing to spend and when to stop, and don’t run swarms in a loop overnight. See CUIDADOS.md (in Portuguese) for details.
Technical section

The kit: for writing the sheet and checking it before /swarm

You don’t need this section to understand what came before.

What it is

A template, a checker, and a helper for choosing

Python 3 with the standard library. No packages to install and no API key: the scripts only read text files.

📝 modelos/FICHA.md

The Swarm Sheet template in Markdown: Task, How to verify, Pieces (### @nome with owner, must not touch, done when), Verifier, Limits, and, optionally, Worker model.

✅ checar.py

Checks 8 items and gives a score from 0–8 with the reasons and how to fix them. --prompt builds the text to paste (only with a score of 7 or higher), --en outputs it in English, and --json is for another program. Exits with code 0 for a score of 7 or higher.

🧭 escolher.py

Chooses subagent, /boost, /swarm, or /teamwork based on the official table. Use flags (--divide-limpo sim, --dificil, --dias, --lateral) or answer questions in the terminal.

How it works

The process, from start to finish

🧠 you · ⚙️ kit script · 🤖 /swarm in Antigravity

🧠⚙️ escolher.py→ 🧠 fill in the sheet→ ⚙️ checar.py→ 🧠 fix it until you reach 7–8→ ⚙️ checar.py --prompt→ 🧠 paste into /swarm→ 🤖 coordinator splits it up→ 🧠 follow the board→ 🤖 verifier runs the test→ 🧠 review and clean up

1. The 8 items

Task · verification command · 2+ pieces · an owner for everyone · non-overlapping owners · must not touch and done when · verifier · limits without an unsupervised loop.

2. Overlap

Same file, a folder containing a file assigned to another piece, and a simple glob (a pattern with * that matches multiple files, such as src/*.js). Always put paths in backticks; an owner without backticks is treated as a question (investigation).

3. Verifier

Missing - roda: fails the check. The verifier-to-worker ratio (1 for every 2–4 pieces) and using the same model as the workers only trigger warnings.

4. Loops

“Run all night without supervision,” “in a loop,” and “dozens of sessions” fail the check. A negation (“no loops”) passes.

From the sheet to text you can paste

Check whether this is a swarm task

Without flags, the script asks 4 questions. With flags, it answers directly. (The scripts print in Portuguese.)

Por quê: Tarefa ampla que se divide limpo em arquivos, pacotes ou perguntas independentes: workers com nome trabalham ao mesmo tempo e um @verifier revisa os diffs e roda os testes, tudo num quadro único.
Plano exigido: Google AI Pro e Google AI Ultra (em prévia; gasta cota muito mais rápido que uma conversa) · horizonte típico: minutos a horas (tabela oficial: https://antigravity.google/docs/swarm)

5

Paste it into Antigravity and follow along

The line already starts with /swarm: paste the whole line into the conversation without typing /swarm first. The documentation shows the command being typed and completed with Tab; if the pasted command isn’t recognized, type /swarm, press Tab, and paste only the rest of the text. In the app, open the panel through the “Open the Swarm UI” link. In the CLI, run /swarm board or press Alt+S. When the coordinator reports back, run the “How to verify” command yourself and ask it to remove the workers that have finished.

/swarm board   # na CLI do Antigravity: abre o quadro ao vivo

Examples

Four ready-to-use sheets, all scoring 8

Each folder has the ficha.md, prompt.txt, and prompt.en.txt generated by checar.py. The real outputs for all examples are in exemplos/saidas.txt (in Portuguese).

🧮 calculadora-roi

One-page calculator with 4 inputs (traffic, conversion, average order value, estimated increase): @layout, @entradas, and @calculo each own their files, and 1 verifier runs the calculation test.

🛬 landing-page

Six pieces (header, benefits, form, testimonials, FAQ, and assembly with the buttons), each section in its own file, a working copy for assembly, and 2 verifiers.

🐢 investigacao-lenta

Three read-only investigators (imports, configuration, and plugins) with questions as their assignments, and a verifier that repeats the measurements and sorts the fixes.

🧪 testes-por-pacote

One worker per broken package, without deleting or weakening assertions, and a verifier that compares the diffs and runs the full test suite.

The slow investigation and per-package testing examples are inspired by examples in the official documentation; the sheets, including the verifier and limits, are part of the kit.
